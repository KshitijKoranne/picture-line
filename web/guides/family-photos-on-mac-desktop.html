How to keep family photos on your Mac desktop

By Kshitij Koranne, 8 October 2026

Your phone is full of photos of the people you love, and your Mac is where you spend the working day. Here are three ways to bring the two together, from the built-in options to something a little warmer.

1. Use a family photo as your wallpaper

This is the quickest route and it's built into macOS.

  1. Open the Apple menu, choose System Settings, then click Wallpaper in the sidebar.
  2. Click Add Photo, Add Folder or Album, and pick a photo or a whole album.

You can also do it from the Photos app: select a photo, click the Share button and choose Set Wallpaper.

Good for: one big, beautiful photo. The catch: the moment you open a window, it's hidden. Busy group shots also make desktop icons hard to read, so pick a calm photo with space in it, like a beach, a sky or a wide landscape.

2. Add the Photos widget to your desktop

Since macOS Sonoma, widgets can live on the desktop.

  1. Control-click an empty spot on the desktop and choose Edit Widgets.
  2. Find Photos in the widget gallery and drag it onto the desktop, then click Done.

The widget shows either featured photos or an album you choose, one picture at a time. If you want one particular photo to stay put, make an album that holds just that photo and point the widget at it.

Good for: a tidy, changing view of your library. The catch: it's a small rectangle showing a single photo, and you don't decide much about how it looks.

3. Hang them on a string with Picture-Line

This is the one I make, so take it with a pinch of salt. Picture-Line hangs a row of photos on a piece of twine across the top of your desktop, behind your windows, each in an instant-photo frame with a wooden clip. You drag photos straight onto it, write a caption underneath, and leave a note on the back.

It feels more like a fridge door than a slideshow. The photos swing a little when your pointer passes, fairy lights come on at sunset, and you can hold ⌃ ⌥ to bring the string forward over your windows for a moment. It's free for up to five photos.

Which photos work best small

  • Close-ups beat group shots. On a desktop, faces read best when they fill most of the frame.
  • Mix in things that aren't faces. A child's drawing, the dog, the street outside your old house. They give the eye somewhere to rest.
  • Keep it to a handful. Five to seven photos feel personal. Twenty feels like a screensaver.
  • Swap them with the seasons. A quick change every month or so stops them fading into the background.
  • Write something. A name, a place or a date turns a photo back into a memory.

Keeping it private

If you share your screen on video calls, remember the desktop can show up when you switch windows. With Picture-Line, choose Hide String from its menu bar icon before a call. Your photos never leave your Mac either way: the wallpaper, the widget and Picture-Line all work locally.
